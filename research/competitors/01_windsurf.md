# Windsurf: Competitive Brief (May 2026)

A deep brief on Windsurf — the agentic IDE formerly known as Codeium, now owned by Cognition (the Devin team). Covers the company's current state, product surface, model stack, pricing, customer base, funding history, and the strategic openings it leaves for a voice/gaze/diffusion-native local-first IDE like eri. Public sources only.

---

## 1. What Windsurf is today (May 2026)

Windsurf is the second-place commercial AI IDE behind Cursor by mindshare and price-tier symmetry, but the first-place player in regulated-industry sales (FedRAMP/HIPAA/ITAR), and now the front-end of Cognition's Devin platform.

Three identities matter to anyone modelling the company:

- **Codeium (2022 – Nov 2024).** A free, then freemium Copilot competitor. The product was a *plugin* — chat plus autocomplete — that lived inside VS Code, JetBrains, Vim, and ~40 other editors [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/plugins/getting-started].
- **Windsurf (Nov 2024 – Jul 2025).** Codeium pivoted to a forked-VS-Code IDE called Windsurf, with the Cascade agent as the centrepiece. The plugin business continued in parallel and is still alive in 2026 [source: https://www.neowin.net/news/codeium-is-now-windsurf-launches-windsurf-plugin-for-jetbrains-to-reach-enterprises/].
- **Windsurf (a Cognition company) (Jul 2025 – present).** After a public failed acquisition by OpenAI and a reverse-acquihire by Google, Cognition bought what remained — IP, brand, ~200 employees, and the customer book — for a reported ~$250M, closed late 2025 [source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/cognition-maker-of-the-ai-coding-agent-devin-acquires-windsurf/; https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/cognition-windsurf-acquisition-swe-1-5-codemaps-2026].

The Cognition acquisition is the dominant fact about Windsurf today. Cognition's bet is that Devin (autonomous cloud agent) and Windsurf (interactive IDE) are two ends of one continuum, and that the winning enterprise pitch is *"plan with the human, hand off to the machine, review and merge."* Windsurf 2.0 (15 April 2026) operationalised that thesis with a Devin tab inside the IDE, a Kanban-style "Agent Command Center" for tracking parallel sessions, and "Spaces" that bundle related agents/PRs/files [source: https://www.testingcatalog.com/windsurf-2-0-adds-devin-and-agent-command-center/; https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/4/windsurf-2-0-has-launched-with-a-new-agent-command-center-spaces-and-devin-integration/].

The post-merger Windsurf scale claims (as of March–May 2026) are roughly:

- ~1M+ monthly active users [source: https://www.getpanto.ai/blog/windsurf-ai-ide-statistics].
- 4,000+ enterprises "in production," up from ~350 at the time of the Cognition deal [source: https://shipper.now/windsurf-stats/].
- Cognition's blended ARR reportedly *doubled* on closing the Windsurf acquisition (Devin was ~$73M ARR mid-2025; Windsurf was reported at ~$82–$100M ARR around the same time), putting the combined company near $200M ARR by late 2025 [source: https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/ai-talent-wars-cognition-windsurf-openai/; https://techfundingnews.com/cognition-ai-25b-valuation-funding-talks-devin-software-engineer/].
- Cognition was valued at ~$10.2B in September 2025 after a $400M raise (two months post-Windsurf), with reports of follow-on talks at $25B in Q1 2026 [source: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/08/cognition-valued-at-10point2-billion-two-months-after-windsurf-.html; https://techfundingnews.com/cognition-ai-25b-valuation-funding-talks-devin-software-engineer/].

In short: a Codeium plugin business → a Cursor-shaped IDE → the front-end for an autonomous-agent company. Each pivot was forced by circumstance more than design, and the product surface still shows the seams.

## 2. Core IDE features

Windsurf is a fork of VS Code, like Cursor. The four surfaces that matter are Cascade (agent), Tab (autocomplete), Inline Edit (Cmd+I-style transforms), and Chat. The order of importance in 2026 marketing is Cascade first, everything else second.

### Cascade

Cascade is Windsurf's agent — the equivalent of Cursor's Composer/Agent surface. Public materials describe it as an "agentic AI assistant" capable of "proactive thinking, planning, and execution" with persistent context awareness across edits, terminal commands, navigation, and clipboard activity [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/cascade/cascade; https://windsurf.com/cascade]. The surface supports:

- **Code mode** (full agentic edit + tool use) and **Chat mode** (read-only Q&A).
- **Built-in planning agent.** A specialised long-horizon planner runs alongside the main coding model, continually refining a plan while the executor takes short-term actions and ticks off in-conversation Todo items [source: https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/windsurf-2-deep-dive-cascade-agents-flows-2026].
- **Auto-lint-fix.** Lint errors generated by Cascade are detected and fixed inside the same turn [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/cascade/cascade].
- **Checkpoints.** Pre-operation snapshots with one-click rollback, same shape as Cursor's checkpoints.
- **Trajectories.** Past Cascade conversations can be shared with teammates and re-injected as context via `@mention` [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/cascade/cascade].
- **Memories.** Persistent facts that carry across sessions — a project-scoped knowledge layer the agent reads on every turn.
- **Workflows.** Reusable, parameterised recipes — Cascade equivalent of saved macros or skills.
- **MCP integration.** Cascade is a first-class MCP client with a marketplace, one-click installs, per-team whitelisting, and a 100-tool cap on the active set [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/cascade/mcp; https://www.mcpbundles.com/blog/windsurf-mcp-tools].
- **Voice input.** Cascade accepts voice prompts natively (a feature Cursor does not match) [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/cascade/cascade].

### Tab (Supercomplete + Autocomplete)

Windsurf's autocomplete surface has two modes [source: https://docs.windsurf.com/tab/overview]:

- **Autocomplete** — classical inline ghost-text. Single-cursor, append-only.
- **Supercomplete** — the headline mode. Floating windows around the cursor propose *deletions and additions* simultaneously, predicting the next *edit* rather than the next *token*. This is Windsurf's analogue to Cursor Tab's "next-edit" model.

Supercomplete grew out of four "Wave" releases on the Codeium codebase: Wave 3 added "tab-to-jump" (move cursor to predicted location), Wave 4 added "tab-to-import" (auto-add imports on dependency change), Wave 5 collapsed those into a single faster model — what shipped as Supercomplete in current Windsurf [source: https://learnprompting.org/blog/windsurf-wave-5; https://dataglobalhub.org/resource/articles/codeium-introduces-windsurf-tab-in-wave-5].

The benchmark Windsurf shops most aggressively against Cursor is autocomplete latency: published numbers around **sub-150 ms** for Tab, generally accepted in third-party reviews as faster than Cursor's tab on equivalent hardware [source: https://www.vibecodingacademy.ai/blog/windsurf-vs-cursor; https://toolradar.com/blog/windsurf-vs-cursor-2026].

### Inline Edit (Cmd+I) and Chat

These are the unsurprising third and fourth surfaces. Inline Edit is a single-shot transform: highlight code, give a natural-language instruction, accept/reject a diff. Chat is what's left of the original Codeium product — a side-panel Q&A surface with file `@mentions`, with all "agentic" capabilities deferred to Cascade. Neither is the surface Windsurf markets.

### Codemaps (the most distinctive feature)

Codemaps, beta-launched in October 2025 and steadily improved through Wave 13 (Dec 2025) and beyond, is Windsurf's most differentiated piece [source: https://cognition.ai/blog/codemaps; https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/codemaps]. A Codemap is a *shareable visual artifact* — a generated diagram of how a codebase actually executes, expressed as Mermaid diagrams, file-execution order, and component relationships. Properties:

- **Bridges human comprehension and agent reasoning.** The same artifact is rendered for the human reviewer and supplied as context to Cascade via `@codemap` [source: https://cognition.ai/blog/codemaps].
- **Shareable as links.** Browser-viewable for teammates. Enterprise teams must opt in because the artifact is server-hosted.
- **"Chat with map" mode.** Hovering or asking about a node sends Cascade a contextual sub-prompt with that node's slice of the codebase.
- **Smart mode.** A higher-effort generation that uses Cascade-style reasoning to label and group nodes by purpose, not just by import graph.

Codemaps is the clearest signal that Cognition is *not* trying to out-Cursor Cursor. It's trying to make the codebase itself a first-class data structure the human and the agent both read. That framing matters for any competitor: it's the most ambitious "context engineering" surface in the market right now.

## 3. The Flow / agent loop

Cursor calls it Composer; Windsurf calls it AI Flow. The architectural difference is real and worth understanding.

Cursor's Composer loop is *turn-driven*. The user types, the agent runs a planning + tool-call loop until verification passes or the turn ends, then waits for the next user message. Cursor's checkpoints and multi-file diffs are per-turn.

Windsurf's Flow is *continuous*. The Cascade context engine watches your actions — edits, terminal commands, navigation, clipboard — and updates its working context *as you work*, not only when you prompt it [source: https://datalakehousehub.com/blog/2026-03-context-management-windsurf/; https://markaicode.com/windsurf-flow-context-engine/]. The metaphor in Windsurf's own marketing is "the AI is on the same page as you, in real time," and the practical implication is that when you finally do issue a prompt, Cascade already knows what you were trying to do.

The architecture has four reasonably-public components [source: https://markaicode.com/windsurf-flow-context-engine/; https://datalakehousehub.com/blog/2026-03-context-management-windsurf/]:

1. **Orchestration layer.** A VS Code base with a routing layer that decides which model handles which sub-task (planner vs executor vs Tab).
2. **Codebase indexer + Fast Context.** A real-time index combined with a Cognition-built "Fast Context" retriever that selects the most relevant code per turn. Fast Context is the closest analogue to Cursor's Turbopuffer+reranker stack, but is more aggressively short-cached for latency.
3. **Specialised planner.** A long-horizon plan model that runs concurrently with the executor; the plan is visible to the user as the in-conversation Todo list.
4. **Verification loop.** Cascade reads terminal output and test output between steps; lint errors generated mid-turn are fixed mid-turn, not punted to the next prompt.

The "Flow" framing is more than marketing. It dictates two design choices that differ materially from Cursor:

- **No hard mode-switch between "Ask" and "Agent."** Cascade decides whether the current action is a read, a write, or a plan refinement based on the running context. This is more fluid but less predictable than Cursor's explicit Plan / Ask / Agent / Manual modes.
- **Heavy reliance on tracked side effects.** Cascade infers intent partly from terminal commands and clipboard activity. That's powerful when it works and disorienting when it doesn't — and is the source of several stability complaints (see §10).

A reasonable summary: Windsurf is trying to make the loop *invisible* to the user; Cursor exposes it. Both are defensible, but a voice/gaze-native IDE like eri inherits more from the Windsurf "ambient" framing than from Cursor's mode-switch framing.

## 4. Models

Windsurf, like Cursor, runs a hybrid of frontier providers + a proprietary in-house family. The in-house line is now the strategic centre of the product.

### The SWE family

- **SWE-1** (May 2025). Codeium's first proprietary model family. Three sizes — SWE-1 (full), SWE-1-lite (mid), SWE-1-mini (autocomplete) — pitched as "frontier-size AI for the full software-engineering lifecycle" with performance competitive with Claude 3.5 Sonnet on tool-use and multi-hop tasks [source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250515138505/en/Windsurf-Launches-SWE-1-A-Frontier-AI-Model-Family-Built-for-the-Full-Software-Engineering-Lifecycle; https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/05/16/windsurf-launches-swe-1-a-frontier-ai-model-family-for-end-to-end-software-engineering/]. SWE-1-lite replaced "Cascade Base" and went unlimited for all users.
- **SWE-1.5** (29 October 2025). Cognition's first model release under the new ownership, and the most aggressive performance positioning the company has ever made. Public claims [source: https://cognition.ai/blog/swe-1-5; https://apidog.com/blog/swe-1-5/; https://www.neowin.net/news/windsurf-wave-13-introduces-the-new-swe-15-model-and-git-worktrees/]:
  - "Frontier-size, hundreds of billions of parameters."
  - SWE-Bench accuracy ~40% — parity with Claude 3.5 Sonnet, well below Composer 2.5's claimed 79.8% on the easier multilingual variant.
  - **~950 tokens/sec served**, 6× faster than Haiku 4.5 and 13× faster than Sonnet 4.5.
  - Trained with end-to-end RL on top of the Cascade harness itself ("we co-designed the model, inference system, and agent harness as one unified system") — the same RL-on-real-tools pattern Cursor uses.
  - Served via Cerebras for inference, with a custom draft model for speculative decoding and request-prioritisation infrastructure built specifically for this model.
- **SWE-1.6** (mentioned on the pricing page as "our latest model" in May 2026, no detailed release post yet found publicly) [source: https://windsurf.com/pricing].

The SWE family's pitch is **speed-first, frontier-class accuracy**. Cognition explicitly trades the absolute top of the benchmark leaderboard for a 6-13× throughput edge, and frames that as the right call for an agentic loop that calls the model dozens of times per task. This is a different bet from Cursor's Composer 2.5, which targets near-Opus accuracy at frontier-fast throughput. Whether SWE-1.5/1.6 "feels" 13× faster in the IDE is genuinely debated in third-party reviews — Cerebras throughput is real but rate-limited, and frontier models still dominate the model picker for most paying users [source: https://www.taskade.com/blog/windsurf-review; https://www.vibecoding.app/blog/windsurf-review].

### Frontier models

Windsurf ships the standard frontier set: Claude (Sonnet, Opus, Haiku 4.x), GPT-5.x family including GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.2-Codex, and Gemini 3 Flash [source: https://windsurf.com/changelog]. The Anthropic relationship has a complicated history — Anthropic cut Windsurf's direct API access in early June 2025 amid the OpenAI-acquisition rumours, with co-founder Jared Kaplan publicly saying *"it would be odd for us to be selling Claude to OpenAI"* [source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/05/anthropic-co-founder-on-cutting-access-to-windsurf-it-would-be-odd-for-us-to-sell-claude-to-openai/; https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/03/windsurf-says-anthropic-is-limiting-its-direct-access-to-claude-ai-models/]. After the Cognition acquisition the relationship has been partially restored, but Claude 4-class models on Windsurf went through a months-long gap that pushed Cognition to accelerate SWE-1.5 and to lean heavily on a BYOK story.

### Tab and Supercomplete models

SWE-1-mini powers Tab; the model is small, MoE-style, optimised for prefill-heavy short-output completion. SWE-1-lite is the default Cascade fallback model and is unlimited on all paid tiers [source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250515138505/en/Windsurf-Launches-SWE-1-A-Frontier-AI-Model-Family-Built-for-the-Full-Software-Engineering-Lifecycle].

## 5. Devin integration

Devin integration is the single biggest product change of the Cognition era and is genuinely without parallel in any other commercial IDE.

### What landed (Windsurf 2.0, 15 April 2026)

[source: https://windsurf.com/blog/windsurf-2-0; https://cognition.ai/blog/devin-in-windsurf; https://www.testingcatalog.com/windsurf-2-0-adds-devin-and-agent-command-center/]

- **Devin in the editor.** A Devin tab inside the IDE. Users plan a task with Cascade locally, then hit one button to *hand off* the plan to a Devin cloud agent that opens a PR on its own VM, with browser/desktop/computer-use capabilities.
- **Agent Command Center.** A Kanban surface showing all in-flight sessions — local Cascade tabs and cloud Devin VMs — grouped by status (planning, running, blocked, review). The reviewer experience is unified: you accept PRs from Cascade and Devin in the same queue.
- **Spaces.** A task-scoped bundle of Cascade sessions, Devin VMs, PRs, files, and shared context. The goal: switching between long-running multi-agent jobs without rebuilding context every time.
- **Devin CLI.** A terminal-based Devin agent ("Devin in Terminal") that runs locally with full access to your codebase and tools, with a seamless escalate-to-cloud flow when a task needs a sandbox VM. *All Windsurf users get the CLI bundled with their existing subscription* — this is the closest Cognition has come to a Claude-Code-style terminal-native experience [source: https://cognition.ai/blog/devin-in-windsurf].
- **Devin Review and Quick Review.** Lightweight Devin invocations from inside Windsurf for diff review and snap fixes, billed against the same Windsurf seat rather than a separate Devin subscription [source: https://windsurf.com/changelog].

The strategic shape is now: **Windsurf is Devin's IDE front-end, and Devin is Windsurf's "background agent" tier.** Cursor has its own Background Agents but they run inside Cursor's harness — there is no equivalent of a dedicated, separately-billed autonomous engineer like Devin sitting behind the agent button. This is a real architectural asymmetry, and the place where Cognition is most clearly playing a different game.

### What it costs

Devin retains its own SKU outside Windsurf. The Devin Core plan is $20/mo + $2.25/ACU (where one ACU ≈ 15 minutes of active Devin work), and the Devin Team plan is $500/mo with 250 ACUs bundled and additional ACUs at ~$2 each [source: https://devin.ai/pricing/; https://costbench.com/software/ai-coding-assistants/devin-ai/; https://venturebeat.com/programming-development/devin-2-0-is-here-cognition-slashes-price-of-ai-software-engineer-to-20-per-month-from-500]. Inside Windsurf, the *Devin CLI* and *Quick Review* invocations are bundled into the Windsurf plan; *full Devin cloud sessions* still bill the user's Devin account if they want long autonomous runs. The two billing rails confuse most first-time users.

## 6. Pricing

Windsurf reset its pricing on 19 March 2026, moving from a credit-based model to a *quota-based* model — and conveniently aligning its Pro tier with Cursor's $20/mo flat fee [source: https://tokenmix.ai/blog/windsurf-quota-pricing-change-2026; https://flexprice.io/blog/windsurf-ai-pricing-breakdown].

| Tier | Price (USD) | What's included | Notable catches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Limited Cascade usage (daily + weekly refresh); unlimited SWE-1-lite for chat + Tab; MCP and most premium features | Tight daily/weekly quota on premium models; no Devin CLI |
| Light | $0 (BYOK) | Unlimited extra usage at provider API pricing; full IDE | You pay your own provider tokens, Windsurf takes the IDE seat for free |
| Pro | $20/mo | Standard usage quota; access to all premium models (GPT-5.x, Claude family, Gemini 3); MCP; Devin CLI + Quick Review; Codemaps | Quota meaningful for power users; overage at API rates |
| Max | $200/mo | "Heavy" quota — Cursor Ultra-tier equivalent; priority routing; Devin Review with larger ACU pool | Same quota model, just bigger |
| Teams | $40/user/mo | Centralized billing, admin dashboard, analytics, team rules and workflows, knowledge base | BYOK not available; per-seat |
| Enterprise | Custom (reports cite ~$60/user/mo entry for ≤200 users, higher with hybrid/FedRAMP) | RBAC, SSO + SCIM, hybrid deploy, FedRAMP/HIPAA/ITAR, account management, custom MCP registry, longer context windows; pooled Agent Compute Units | No BYOK; sales-led; ACU model |

[source: https://www.verdent.ai/guides/windsurf-pricing-2026; https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/windsurf-pricing/; https://docs.windsurf.com/windsurf/accounts/usage]

### BYOK story

Windsurf's BYOK is strictly *better* than Cursor's:

- **Free and Pro users can use BYOK keys**, and BYOK requests *bypass the quota system entirely* — Windsurf charges you the seat only, your provider charges you the tokens [source: https://flexprice.io/blog/windsurf-ai-pricing-breakdown].
- **Cascade itself works under BYOK on Pro.** Unlike Cursor — which explicitly blocks Agent/Edit on BYOK because they rely on Cursor-fine-tuned models — Windsurf permits Cascade against your own Anthropic/OpenAI/Google key. SWE-1.5 obviously can't run on BYOK (it's not a public model), but the agent loop is not bricked.
- **Teams and Enterprise do not support BYOK** [source: https://devtoolsreview.com/pricing/windsurf-pricing/].

This is the cleanest pricing differentiator Windsurf has against Cursor. Heavy users who hate the credit-pool surprise can predictably move to BYOK on Windsurf in a way they cannot on Cursor.

### Enterprise pricing detail

Enterprise plans are denominated in **Agent Compute Units (ACUs)** rather than dollars or requests, matching Devin's billing unit. ACU consumption scales with model selection and turn length — frontier models burn ACUs faster than SWE-1.5. This is also how Cognition is hedging the gross-margin problem that's nearly bankrupted the Cursor tier model: ACUs decouple the seat price from the actual cost of inference, giving Cognition room to push users toward SWE-1.5 when frontier models would be unprofitable [source: https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/windsurf-pricing/; https://brainroad.com/devin-pricing-in-2026-real-cost-hidden-spend-and-alternatives/].

## 9. Strengths vs Cursor

Where Windsurf wins outright in May 2026:

1. **Enterprise compliance.** FedRAMP High, DoD IL4/IL5, ITAR, HIPAA BAA. Cursor has SOC2 and nothing else — Cursor is unable to bid for public-sector, defence, or most US healthcare work. This is the cleanest single differentiator on the board [source: https://securityideals.com/learn/blog/cursor-ide-vs-windsurf-security; https://harini.blog/2025/07/02/ai-coding-assistants-comparing-cursor-vs-windsurf-for-healthcare-enterprise-readiness/].
2. **Hybrid + on-prem deployment.** Windsurf supports hybrid + FedRAMP-managed deploys via Palantir FedStart; Cursor remains cloud-only [source: https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-x4iqsqorbfaj4].
3. **IDE breadth via plugins.** Windsurf still ships first-party plugins for JetBrains (IntelliJ, PyCharm, WebStorm, GoLand), Vim/NeoVim, Visual Studio, Jupyter, and Chrome [source: https://windsurf.com/plugins; https://docs.windsurf.com/plugins/getting-started]. Cursor is fork-only — if you live in JetBrains or Vim, Cursor isn't available.
4. **Codemaps.** No equivalent in Cursor. The visual + shareable + agent-readable codebase artifact is genuinely novel and the most-shipped piece of "context engineering UX" in the market.
5. **Devin integration.** Cursor has Background Agents but no equivalent of a separately-engineered autonomous engineer with its own VM, browser, and computer-use capabilities, wired into the same review queue.
6. **BYOK that doesn't cripple the agent.** Cascade runs on user-supplied Anthropic/OpenAI/Google keys; Cursor's Composer is locked to Cursor billing. For heavy Pro users, the cost predictability gap is large.
7. **Autocomplete latency.** Sub-150 ms Supercomplete is faster than Cursor Tab in head-to-head reviews [source: https://www.vibecodingacademy.ai/blog/windsurf-vs-cursor].
8. **SWE-1.5 throughput.** ~950 tok/s on Cerebras is unmatched. Whether *quality at that speed* matches Composer 2.5 is debated, but the raw-throughput pitch is unique.

## 10. Weaknesses

Where Windsurf loses or is structurally weaker:

1. **Reliability and stability.** The most consistent complaint across reviews and the project's own GitHub: IDE freezes (a documented bug had Cascade messages stalling the event loop for ~5 minutes), Supercomplete suggestions that silently delete working code, context selection that only picks "partial files" from a folder even when explicitly told otherwise [source: https://github.com/Exafunction/codeium/issues/284; https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/windsurf-review/; https://docs.windsurf.com/troubleshooting/windsurf-common-issues].
2. **Hallucination on imports.** Cascade is widely reported to invent non-existent imports and supplement code with logic that has nothing to do with the request — more frequently than Cursor in like-for-like tests [source: https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/windsurf-review/].
3. **Frontier model coverage gaps.** The Anthropic cutoff in mid-2025 left months where Claude 4-class models weren't directly usable on Windsurf. Even today, Claude availability lags Cursor's, and the BYOK workaround puts the cost back on the user.
4. **Brand whiplash and identity.** Codeium → Windsurf → Cognition-owned Windsurf inside ~18 months. The founders are at a different company. Documentation, billing terminology (credits → quotas → ACUs), and product surfaces (Cascade, Supercomplete, Codemaps, Devin in Windsurf) shift release-by-release. Enterprise champions get tired of re-explaining the product to security review.
5. **Two billing rails.** Windsurf seats + Devin ACUs is genuinely confusing. New users routinely don't understand which surface debits which budget.
6. **Smaller model leadership.** SWE-1.5 matches Sonnet 3.5 on SWE-Bench — ~40%. Composer 2.5 claims ~79.8% on the multilingual variant. Whatever the apples-to-apples comparison, the perception that Cursor is "the place with the smarter in-house model" persists. Cognition's defence is throughput, not accuracy, and that messaging is harder to land in benchmarks bake-offs.
7. **Pivot velocity exhausts users.** Windsurf 2.0 changed the centre of gravity from Cascade-the-agent to Devin-the-cloud-engineer in one release. Users who picked Windsurf to avoid an autonomous-agent-first product now have one bolted on by default.
8. **No model dropdown advantage.** Both products expose roughly the same frontier set. Windsurf doesn't out-route Cursor on model choice; Cursor's Auto router is arguably tighter.
9. **Tab-vs-Composer integration is shallower.** Cursor's Tab and Composer share an editor buffer; Windsurf's Tab (Supercomplete) and Cascade do too, but Cascade's "ambient" context engine and Tab's predictive model don't share state cleanly — Tab can suggest a deletion of code Cascade just wrote, mid-Cascade-turn.

## 11. What eri could learn or borrow

Specific, copyable ideas:

1. **Codemaps as a shared-context artifact.** A generated map of how the code *executes* — not how it imports — usable by both the human and the agent, shareable as a link, addressable via `@`. eri's voice + gaze surfaces benefit even more from this: the user can literally *look at* a node and ask about it. **Borrow the concept, not the implementation.**
2. **Continuous context tracking ("Flow").** The Cascade context engine watches edits, terminal, navigation, clipboard, and updates working memory in real time. For eri, gaze data is the highest-bandwidth equivalent of this — passive intent signal we should be folding into context the same way Cascade folds in terminal output.
3. **Planner + executor as two cooperating models.** A continuously-refining plan model alongside a short-action executor model gives a smoother UX than a single-model loop. This is also a natural place to use a slower, more accurate diffusion-style model for the plan and a fast streaming model for the executor.
4. **BYOK that doesn't brick the agent.** Lock proprietary fine-tuned models to your billing, but let users run Cascade-equivalent agents against their own provider keys. This is the single most-praised feature Windsurf has over Cursor among power users.
5. **MCP marketplace with team whitelisting and a per-session tool cap.** Cascade's 100-tool active cap is a smart guardrail against context bloat. Worth copying verbatim.
6. **Memories as a project-scoped layer.** Persistent facts referenced on every turn — eri's gaze logs and voice transcripts are an obvious feed for this, but the data structure itself is a good design.
7. **Agent Command Center.** A Kanban surface for parallel agents (local + remote) with status grouping is a clean UX pattern. eri's "iterator panel" is already in this neighbourhood; the Kanban grouping by status is the missing visual.
8. **Quota-not-credit billing.** Users hate the surprise. A flat quota that resets weekly/daily is simpler to reason about and reduces churn, even at the same average price.
9. **Co-design model + harness + inference.** Cognition's framing of SWE-1.5 as "we co-designed the model, the harness, and the inference system as one product" is the right framing for an in-house model. Anyone shipping a custom code model should be doing harness-aware RL, not single-turn RL.

## 12. Wedges eri can exploit against Windsurf

Where Windsurf is structurally weak and a voice/gaze/diffusion-native local-first IDE can press:

1. **Local-first by default.** Windsurf is unambiguously cloud-first. Even Enterprise FedRAMP deploys are cloud, just inside GovCloud. eri's eri_coreutils sidecar gives us a credible "your code never leaves your laptop unless you say so" pitch that no Cursor or Windsurf SKU can match. This is the wedge in privacy-sensitive teams that are *not* FedRAMP — solo developers, security researchers, regulated startups too small for Palantir-fronted compliance.
2. **Voice as a first-class input, not a checkbox.** Cascade has voice input as a checkbox; Cursor doesn't really. eri's voice surface — paired with gaze for disambiguation — is a different product, not the same product with a microphone glued on. Demo videos win here.
3. **Gaze as ambient context.** Windsurf reads terminal, clipboard, navigation. eri can read *where the user is looking* — a strictly stronger signal for what code they want changed. No competitor has this.
4. **Diffusion-style edit models.** The current generation of code-edit transformers (including SWE-1.5 and Composer 2.5) emit tokens left-to-right. Diffusion-style edit models change the cost curve of "rewrite this whole region" by an order of magnitude. Windsurf cannot adopt this without rebuilding their inference stack; eri can build around it from day one. (Cognition has a Cerebras lock-in on SWE-1.5; we don't.)
5. **One billing rail.** Windsurf has Windsurf seats *and* Devin ACUs. eri can ship one number, no surprise, and call out the competitor's two-rail confusion explicitly.
6. **No autonomous-engineer-by-default.** Some developers actively don't want a cloud VM running a separately-billed AI engineer in the background. Windsurf 2.0 pushed Devin to the front; eri can position as "the agentic IDE for people who actually code." This is a smaller market but a defensible one.
7. **Predictable performance.** Reliability complaints are Windsurf's biggest open wound. A stable IDE that doesn't freeze for five minutes on a Cascade send is a credible product positioning, especially against a competitor with a steady stream of stability bugs in its own changelog.
8. **No founder risk.** Windsurf's original founders are at Google DeepMind. The current team is rebuilding under a new parent company. eri is single-founder-led with no acquisition pressure — a real comfort for enterprise champions who got burned in the Codeium → Windsurf → Cognition arc.
9. **Plugin coverage as a backstop, not a strategy.** Windsurf's JetBrains/Vim plugins exist but are demoted to second-class in marketing relative to the IDE fork. eri can ship a thinner, faster sidecar that lives equally well inside any host editor — including Cursor and Windsurf themselves — and use that as a Trojan-horse install path against both.

## Open questions

- **SWE-1.6 specifications.** Mentioned on the pricing page as "our latest model" but no detailed release post found. Parameter count, training method, SWE-Bench numbers, and pricing all unknown publicly.
- **Cerebras lock-in on SWE-1.5.** If Cognition is co-designed around Cerebras inference, can they serve SWE-1.5/1.6 on other accelerators if Cerebras throughput becomes a bottleneck or a price negotiation lever? Public materials don't say.
- **BYOK economics under Pro.** Windsurf claims BYOK Cascade is unlimited at API cost. Whether Cognition rate-limits BYOK requests on the back end to protect their own queue is not publicly stated.
- **Devin CLI vs Claude Code positioning.** Cognition is building a terminal-native agent in parallel with Anthropic's. How much do they share — system-prompt structure, tool surface, file-edit semantics — is not public.
- **Gross margin under SWE-1.5.** Cerebras throughput is real but Cerebras pricing is opaque. Whether SWE-1.5 actually moves Cognition into positive gross margin on Pro (the way Composer 2.5 reportedly did for Cursor) is unverifiable from outside.
- **Long-term plan for the plugin business.** Windsurf still ships JetBrains/Vim plugins. Whether those keep parity with the Windsurf IDE features (Cascade, Codemaps, Devin) over the next 12 months, or quietly atrophy, is the key signal for whether Cognition is committing to multi-IDE or fork-only.
- **Spaces persistence model.** Spaces bundle agents + PRs + files + context. How long is that context retained server-side, how is it scoped under FedRAMP, and is there a local-only mode? Not documented publicly.
- **Anthropic relationship status.** Whether Windsurf has full Claude 4-class direct API access in May 2026 or is still relying on indirect routing / BYOK is unclear from public materials.
