# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Individuals who want **one** AI that actually runs their digital life — not another chat tab.
- **The eri user** — operates their own computer hands-free (control apps, read the screen, voice), and reaches the same eri by texting it (iMessage) when away from the machine. Wants their day *done*, not advice about it.
- **The eri builder** — the same person in Build mode: plans, edits across files, runs tests, ships. One surface, expanded.
Context: a capable person juggling a laptop + phone, tired of copy-pasting between an AI and their actual apps. They reach the site curious-but-skeptical ("does this really *do* things, or just talk?").

## Product Purpose

eri is **Engineered Relative Intelligence (ERI)**, from **Eri Labs — an AGI lab**. The thesis: AGI won't arrive as one universal mind for everyone; it grows **one person at a time**. eri begins as a genuinely capable agent — it uses your computer hands-free, reaches 1,000+ of your apps, texts you from your pocket, builds your software, and remembers you across every device — and, by learning only *you*, grows into your other half. "Relative" means relative *to you* (personalized) and your *relative* (kin). Posture: **an AGI lab, and eri is becoming it** — bold mission, honest product. The AGI/"grows into you"/feelings story is framed as the trajectory ("becomes", "grows into"); present-tense claims stay true to what it does today. Never state present-tense that eri *is* AGI or has feelings *now*. Success: a visitor feels the ambition ("this is an AGI built for me, and it's real today"), believes it, and downloads it.

## Brand Personality

Personal, capable, quietly confident. eri talks like a real person (no "As an AI", no bullet-reflex, no hype), shows rather than claims, and states plainly what it literally does. The marketing playbook is the best **personal-AI + computer-use** companies — *demonstrations over decks*, the phone/iMessage hook, frictionless onboarding — but eri's wedge is the differentiator: it runs your *own machine* hands-free and carries *one memory* across devices. Confidence comes from showing the work, not adjectives about it.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI-SaaS**: cream/white backgrounds, gradient blobs, six identical icon+heading feature cards, "your AI assistant" clichés. The default AI-startup look.
- **Enterprise/corporate**: stock team photos, navy-and-gray, "enterprise-grade" buzzword decks, a dashboard-screenshot hero.
- **Chatbot landing**: a big chat-bubble hero or "talk to our AI" framing that flattens eri into one more chatbot instead of an OS for your computer.
(Apple-grade polish is welcome; the faceless Apple *template* is not.)

## Design Principles

1. **Show eri doing real things.** Live, specific product demos (eri clicking through an app, replying to an email, texting you, connecting a service) beat any feature list. If a section claims a capability, it demonstrates it.
2. **One brain, many surfaces.** Every section reinforces that the laptop eri and the phone eri are the *same* eri with one memory — continuity is the story, not two products.
3. **Practice what you preach.** The site itself should feel engineered and precise (the architecture-report craft, real schematics) because that's what eri is. The medium proves the message.
4. **Honest, pre-launch.** No fake testimonials, no SaaS vanity metrics, no invented logos. The capability sells; the copy is specific, never aspirational filler.
5. **The download is the destination.** Every path resolves to getting eri on the visitor's machine (download → log in & sync → hotkey). The site's job is to make that the obvious next move.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA on a dark surface (body text ≥4.5:1, large/UI ≥3:1 — no light-gray-on-near-black). Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative (the product demos especially must degrade to a static, legible state, never a blank reveal). Full keyboard navigation; focus-visible states; don't encode meaning in the lime accent alone.
